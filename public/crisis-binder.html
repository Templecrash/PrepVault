Introduction

Why this binder

Most emergency plans live in a person's head, a notebook, or a cloud app. When the grid goes down, so does the cloud — and so does the app that needed a connection to be useful.

This workbook puts your plan where it still works: on paper you control, plus an encrypted offline digital copy you keep yourself. The strongest crisis binders share the same shape, and this one is built that way:

  • Dual format — printable, waterproof-friendly pages plus an encrypted offline digital copy
  • Fill-in worksheets, not static lists — the plan is the blank you actually complete
  • An update cadence — quarterly, and after every near-miss or life change
  • Per-section ownership — someone in charge of every section, with an alternate

Fill in every field. If a section doesn't apply to your household, write N/A and date it, so it's clear it was considered and skipped on purpose. Then tell your household where the binder lives and who the out-of-area contact is.

Getting started

How to assemble your binder

  1. Print two copies. One stays in the home. One lives off-site — in a go-bag, vehicle, or outbuilding — so a plan survives even if the house doesn't.
  2. Protect the paper. Use sheet protectors in a 3-ring binder, or print on waterproof paper. Keep the off-site copy in a waterproof pouch.
  3. Build the digital copy. After the paper is filled in, create an encrypted offline copy (Section 9). The cloud is optional. An encrypted local copy is not.
  4. Assign owners. Complete the Ownership page on the next sheet. Every section gets a primary and an alternate.
  5. Set the cadence. Put a quarterly review in your calendar now. Also review within a week of any move, medical change, major purchase, or near-miss.
  6. Tell your people. Household members and your out-of-area contact should know where the binder is and who holds the digital copy.

Section 9

Digital Backup & Security

Purpose: Make a second, encrypted copy of this binder that isn't the only copy, isn't readable by anyone who finds it, and isn't dependent on the cloud.

Owner:  Alternate:

Why the digital copy matters

The paper binder is your primary copy. The digital copy is your backup. Sensitive-document guidance from authoritative sources supports a specific approach:

  • Keep a local copy of important data — data that exists only in the cloud can be lost (CISA).
  • For physical backups, use an encrypted external drive; if you use cloud storage, encrypt files before uploading (California Privacy Protection Agency, April 2026).
  • Encrypt before uploading, and be aware that cloud providers may be able to access customer data and that privacy terms can be unclear (HKCERT).
  • Store an encrypted thumb drive in a filing cabinet, safe, or go-bag — cloud storage always carries some risk because files sit on someone else's server (PIRG).
  • A disaster binder's digital backup should be encrypted and password-protected (FEEA).

The rule in practice: 3-2-1

  • 3 copies of the binder — paper, encrypted drive A, encrypted drive B
  • 2 formats — print and digital
  • 1 copy off-site — in the go-bag, safe, or outbuilding
