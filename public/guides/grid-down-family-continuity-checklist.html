Grid-Down Family Continuity Checklist: A Time-Phased Plan to Keep Your Family Safe, Fed, and Reunited

A complete, time-phased checklist for the hours, days, and weeks after the power goes out — covering water, food, generator safety, medical continuity, and family reunification, with the facts mapped to FEMA, CDC, and Red Cross guidance.

By PrepVault· Last updated August 18, 2026· ENCRYPTED · OFFLINE · NO CLOUD DEPENDENCY

What is a grid-down family continuity checklist?

A grid-down family continuity checklist is a time-phased plan that removes decision-making under stress. In the first hours it covers reuniting your family, securing water and food, and managing power safely. Over the following days and weeks it covers rationing, sanitation, medical continuity, communication, and — if the outage runs long — adapting to a life without the grid. This guide is the complete version, organized by time phase, with source-linked facts, calculators, and the household-specific details most checklists leave out.

Water
1 gallon per person per day (2 in hot climates) for drinking + sanitation.
Fridge / Freezer
Fridge ~4 hours closed · full freezer ~48 hours · discard food at ≥40°F.
Generator
Outdoors only, ≥20 ft from windows and doors — never indoors.
Food order
Eat fridge first (days 1–2), then freezer (days 2–4), then shelf-stable.
Reunification
Pre-agreed meeting place + one out-of-area contact every member can reach.
Reality check
A multi-day outage is a normal baseline — some households were without power 6+ days in January 2026.

01Phase 1 — The First 72 Hours

The first three days decide how much of a problem you actually have. Do these in order, under stress, so you never have to think about what comes next.

🕐

Immediate actions — hours 0 to 72

ACCOUNT · WATER · FOOD · POWER · COMMS · MEDICAL
1
Account for every family member

Locate everyone in the household and any family members who were out. Go to your pre-agreed meeting place rather than waiting for phones to work — cell networks jam or fail during outages.

2
Figure out the outage scope

Check whether it is a local breaker fault, a neighborhood outage, or something bigger (EMP vs. an ordinary outage changes everything). Check neighbors, then listen to a battery or hand-crank NOAA weather radio for official updates.

3
Secure water immediately

Fill bathtubs, sinks, and every clean container right away while municipal pressure still holds. Plan on 1 gallon per person per day for drinking and sanitation — more in hot weather.

4
Protect your food

Keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed. A closed fridge holds ~4 hours, a full freezer ~48 hours. Eat refrigerator food first, then freezer, then shelf-stable.

5
Light and power safely

Use flashlights, not candles, to avoid fire. If you run a generator, it goes outside, at least 20 feet from windows and doors — never indoors or in a garage.

6
Protect communications

Conserve phone battery (airplane mode). A corded landline may still work when VoIP and cell fail. Use the NOAA radio and wireless emergency alerts for official information.

7
Handle medical needs first

Move refrigerated medications into a cooler with ice packs, and plan power for any medical devices. If you rely on powered medical equipment, your utility should have you flagged for priority restoration.

8
Cash and documents

ATMs and card readers go down with the grid. Keep small bills on hand and waterproof copies of IDs, insurance, and financial records where you can grab them.

9
Shut off utilities only if needed

Know where your main water shutoff and gas valve are, and turn them off only if you see damage or suspect a leak. A wrench or pliers should be part of your kit.

10
Set a heating or cooling plan

In cold weather, drain pipes or keep a slow drip, and use a wood stove safely. In heat, plan shade, hydration, and how to keep vulnerable family members cool.

02Phase 2 — Stabilization (Days 3–7)

By day three you are past reaction and into management. The job now is making what you have last and keeping everyone healthy.

📆

Ration, rotate, and coordinate

RATIONING · SANITATION · FUEL · COMMUNITY
1
Ration water and food

Stretch the 1-gallon-per-person daily allowance and move through food in the safe order — fridge, then freezer, then shelf-stable — so nothing spoils before it is eaten.

2
Cycle the generator intelligently

Run it in cycles (for example 4 hours on, 4 off) and prioritize the refrigerator and freezer. Generator fuel is finite — ration it aggressively.

3
Manage sanitation

If water pressure is gone, toilets stop flushing. Have garbage bags with ties, towelettes, and a sanitation plan before you need them.

4
Treat and source more water

Boil water (rolling boil), or disinfect with the right bleach concentration. Rotate stored water about every six months so it is always usable.

5
Coordinate with your community

Check on neighbors, especially older adults. Exchange information and supplies. A functioning neighborhood is a family's best multi-week asset.

6
Reassess for the long haul

If there is still no restoration timeline, switch from "waiting it out" to the multi-week plan below. Top off vehicle tanks early — fuel stations cannot pump without power.

03Phase 3 — Multi-Week Adaptation (Day 7 and Beyond)

After a week, a grid-down event becomes a different problem: not "when does power return" but "how do we live until it does." These are the systems to build.

🌱

Build systems for an extended outage

WATER SOURCING · FOOD PRODUCTION · HEAT · CONTINUITY
1
Establish a renewable water source

Electric well pumps stop working without power. Set up a hand pump or a gravity-fed source, and keep treatment (boil or bleach) part of the routine.

2
Produce and preserve food

Shift to the garden, hunting, and fishing, and budget calories carefully. Without refrigeration, preserve what you catch or grow by smoking or salting.

3
Secure heat and cooking

A wood stove becomes your primary heat and cook surface. Never burn charcoal or run a generator or camp stove indoors — carbon monoxide is odorless and deadly.

4
Maintain medical and legal continuity

Keep a 7-day supply of essential prescriptions (30 days for older adults), and know how to reach care and refills. Keep financial and legal documents accessible and waterproof.

5
Trade skills, not just goods

Barter and mutual aid — food, fuel, medical help, and repair skills — matter more than cash in a long outage. Build those relationships now, not after the grid fails.

A generic list fails exactly the households that need it most. Add these per-household items before you need them.

Powered medical equipmentA battery backup or generator plan, and notify your utility for priority restoration.
PetsFood, water, medications, and restraint or carriers for the same duration you plan for yourself.
Extreme heat / coldExtra water in heat; wood, warm bedding, and pipe-freeze protection in cold.

06Family Reunification Plan

When networks fail, the top practice is a plan you agreed on before the phones died. Set this up once and rehearse it.

The four parts of a family reunification plan
PartWhat to decide
Local meeting placeA spot everyone can walk to — a neighbor's house or a landmark near home.
Out-of-area meeting placeA destination outside the affected area if you are separated and local travel is impossible.
Out-of-area contactOne person outside your region that every family member can call or text to relay status — calls outside an outage area often work when local ones do not.
Contact cascadeThe order in which each member contacts the others, so nobody is missed and nobody calls twice.

Write the plan on a card every family member carries, and practice it at least once. A reunification plan that lives only in your head is not a plan.

07Frequently Asked Questions

How much water per person per day for a power outage?

Store 1 gallon of water per person per day for drinking and sanitation, and 2 gallons per person per day if your household includes children, nursing mothers, or someone who is ill, or if you live in a hot climate. FEMA recommends a supply covering several days, and the CDC advises preparing for at least 3 days and up to 2 weeks.

How long is food safe in a fridge without power?

A closed refrigerator keeps food cold for about 4 hours, and a full freezer for about 48 hours (24 hours if half full). Discard refrigerated food that has been at or above 40°F for 2 hours or more, and frozen food that has thawed above 40°F. Eat refrigerator food first, then freezer food, then shelf-stable food.

What size generator do I need for essentials?

Add up the running watts of the essentials you must power — a refrigerator (about 700 W), a freezer (about 700 W), a furnace blower (about 600 W), and a few lights (about 300 W) total roughly 2,300 W — then add about 20–30% for startup surge. Most households can run those essentials on a 5,000–7,500-watt portable generator. Check the rating label on each appliance because actual wattage varies.

How far should a generator be from my house?

Run a portable generator outdoors, at least 20 feet from windows, doors, and vents, and never inside a home, garage, or enclosed space. Carbon monoxide from a generator is odorless and can be fatal, so install working battery-powered CO detectors and keep the generator dry and away from open windows.

What medications need cold storage in a power outage?

Insulin and some liquid antibiotics and biologics must stay refrigerated. Keep a 7-day supply of all essential prescriptions on hand (the Red Cross advises up to 30 days for older adults), know which medications require refrigeration, and plan a cooler with ice packs for them. If you use powered medical equipment, notify your utility so you are flagged for priority restoration.

How do I make a family reunification plan?

Choose a local meeting place (a neighbor's home or a landmark near home) and an out-of-area meeting place, designate one out-of-area contact who every family member can call or text, and agree on the order in which you contact each other. Practice the plan, and write it on a card every family member carries, because cell networks often fail or jam during an outage.
